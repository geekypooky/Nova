Title: 5 ways teens with ADHD take risks — and why

Key points:
- Teens with ADHD are more likely to take risks and act impulsively due to executive function delays (trouble with self-control, flexible thinking, and resisting temptation).
- Low self-esteem can drive risky behavior. Mentors and focusing on strengths can help.
- Mental health issues (anxiety, depression) co-occurring with ADHD can increase the likelihood of risky behavior.
- ADHD medication abuse: Teens may face peer pressure to sell or give out their stimulant medications.
- Teens may not be cautious in the moment, so parents need to discuss ways to be safe in advance (e.g. using protection if sexually active) and talk through consequences before the situation arises.
