Title: ADHD and avoidance
Dr. J breaks down the real science behind ADHD avoidance: how the nervous system mistakes tasks for threats, why perfectionism makes starting feel risky, and what actually happens in your brain before a shutdown. Plus, practical strategies to interrupt the avoidance loop without shame, force, or another failed to-do list.

Key points:
- Avoidance is steering away from something that feels uncomfortable, effortful, uncertain, or emotionally loaded, even if it's something you want to do.
- It's a task initiation problem related to executive dysfunction, not laziness.
- Nervous system interprets demands as threats, activating a stress response (shutdown, irritability).
- Avoidance snowballs: an avoided task gets heavier over time.
- Strategies: Commit to 2-5 minutes, use body doubling, check for physical/emotional overwhelm first, separate identity from behavior (remove shame), change language from "have to" to "choosing to", and build sustainable structures (visual cues, pairing tasks with dopamine).
