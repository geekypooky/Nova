Title: ADHD and sleep: 5 strategies to finally beat insomnia
Sleep expert Marlee Boyle explains why ADHD brains are wired against a good night’s rest.

Key points:
- Up to 80% of people with ADHD struggle with sleep, often due to a delayed circadian rhythm (usually delayed by 90 minutes).
- People with ADHD are often highly sensitive to light, which affects melatonin production.
- Practical tools: Wear amber-lensed glasses 2-3 hours before bed to advance the circadian rhythm and block blue light. Use contoured eye masks to ensure complete darkness without sensory irritation.
- CBT-I (Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Insomnia): Address maladaptive sleep beliefs (cognitive restructuring). E.g., bad sleep one night doesn't guarantee a bad next day.
- Avoid wearables that claim to track sleep stages; they are inaccurate and cause "orthosomnia".
- The 20/20 Rule: If awake in bed for 20 minutes, get up and do a boring, low-light task for 20 minutes (e.g., folding laundry) to reset.
- Hormonal changes like perimenopause or luteal phase (pre-period) heavily disrupt sleep, exacerbating ADHD symptoms.
- Melatonin should be used as a chronobiotic to reset the clock (taken 10-12 hours before wake time), not as a bedtime sedative.
- Avoid long-term use of sedating antihistamines (Benadryl, Unisom)—they cause sedation, not restorative REM sleep, worsening executive function.
