Title: Sorry, I Missed This: Why money fights are never really about money
Financial therapist Dr. Christine Hargrove and Cate Osborn break down why ADHD makes money so hard — and why money fights are rarely about the money itself.

Key points:
- People with ADHD face immense stress regarding money (tracking, forecasting, impulse spending) and carry a heavy burden of shame, assuming that poor money management equals a character deficit.
- Fights about money are rarely about dollars; they are about profound senses of safety, control, independence, and autonomy.
- Financial Self-Efficacy: The belief in your ability to manage your finances. It is lower in people with ADHD, but it is critically important. Improving self-efficacy (e.g. celebrating small wins like paying a bill) improves overall financial well-being for both partners.
- People with ADHD often give great financial advice to others but struggle to apply it to themselves because they discount anything but the "neurotypical way" of managing money. Hiring help (like an ADHD-friendly accountant) is a valid accommodation, not a failure.
- A four-step partnership plan to reduce conflict: 1) Information discovery (transparency without judgment), 2) Organization (categorizing), 3) Analysis (looking for patterns), 4) Planning and problem-solving (testing interventions as pilots). Do NOT jump from discovery to fixing.
- "Court your finances": Build a relationship slowly. Don't hyperfocus on a two-day finance binge, as the task transition cost will make you avoid it later. Limit check-ins to 10-15 minutes.
